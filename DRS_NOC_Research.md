# Equity Analyst Research: Leonardo DRS (DRS) & Northrop Grumman (NOC)

## PART 1 — LEONARDO DRS (DRS)

---

### 1. Why DRS Had No Public Share Price Until 2022

DRS was **taken private in October 2008** when Italian defense conglomerate **Finmeccanica S.p.A.** (now Leonardo S.p.A.) acquired it for **$5.2 billion ($81/share, 100% cash)** and delisted it from the NYSE. It remained a wholly owned private subsidiary — with no public float and no exchange-listed share price — for 14 years.

**Full Timeline:**

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1968 | Founded as DRS (Diagnostic/Retrieval Systems, Inc.) by two engineers from Loral Corporation |
| 1981 | First IPO on NYSE; grew via acquisitions to >$1B revenue by 2004 |
| Oct 2008 | Acquired by Finmeccanica S.p.A. for $5.2B; delisted from NYSE → wholly private |
| Mar 2021 | Traditional IPO filed (targeting ~$670M, 31.9M shares at $20–22) — **withdrawn** due to unfavorable market conditions |
| Nov 29, 2022 | Re-listed on **Nasdaq via reverse merger with RADA Electronic Industries** (Israeli defense electronics firm); combined EV ~$3B; opened at $11.50 under ticker "DRS" |
| Dec 2023 | Voluntarily delisted from Tel Aviv Stock Exchange; Nasdaq only |

**Ownership post-merger**: Leonardo S.p.A. retained ~80.5% (now ~72.3%); RADA shareholders received ~19.5%.

**Bottom line for the report**: The 14-year gap (2008–2022) in share price data exists because DRS was a 100%-owned private subsidiary of a foreign parent — not publicly traded.

### 2. The 2017 Restructuring — What Changed

The "2017 restructuring" was primarily a **rebranding and governance event**, not a standalone operational overhaul. It was the culmination of a multi-year transformation:

**2012–2016 — Operational Restructuring (the real restructuring):**
- CEO **William J. Lynn III** (former U.S. Deputy Secretary of Defense) appointed in 2012
- Workforce cut from **~10,000 to ~5,500** through layoffs and divestitures
- Non-core assets divested; focus narrowed to 8 core defense electronics segments (advanced sensing, tactical power, EW, etc.)
- HQ relocated to Arlington, Virginia
- Parent company "One Company" plan (2015–2016): Finmeccanica merged all subsidiaries (AgustaWestland, Alenia Aermacchi, DRS, Selex ES, OTO Melara, WASS) into one industrial entity with five divisions

**January 1, 2017 — The "Restructuring" Event:**
- Finmeccanica S.p.A. formally renamed itself **Leonardo S.p.A.** (after Leonardo da Vinci)
- **DRS Technologies rebranded as "Leonardo DRS"** to align with the new parent identity
- Integrated into Leonardo's Electronics division
- U.S. FOCI (Foreign Ownership, Control and Influence) mitigations with DoD maintained

**2017 Financial Context:**
- Leonardo group revenue fell: €12.0B (2016) → €11.5B (2017)
- Net income halved: €507M → €274M
- Management launched "Leonardo 2.0" industrial plan in response

**Key Changes Summary:**

| What Changed | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | DRS Technologies → Leonardo DRS |
| Parent Brand | Finmeccanica S.p.A. → Leonardo S.p.A. |
| Structure | Integrated into "One Company" divisional model |
| Divestitures | Non-core transport, logistics, power assets sold off |
| Workforce | Reduced from ~10,000 to ~5,500 |
| Focus | Narrowed to advanced sensing, EW, propulsion, network computing |

### 3. Why DRS Is a Comparable Company to NOC

Despite the significant scale difference (~$3.2B DRS revenue vs. ~$41B NOC revenue), DRS and NOC are genuine peer comparables:

**A. Shared Business Domain**
Both are **pure-play U.S. DoD defense electronics and advanced systems** companies. Neither has meaningful commercial exposure. Both operate under cost-plus and fixed-price government contract structures.

**B. Direct Segment Overlap**

| DRS Segment | NOC Equivalent | Overlapping Capabilities |
|---|---|---|
| Advanced Sensing & Computing (ASC) — ~65% of DRS revenue | **Mission Systems** (NOC's largest segment) | Radar, EO/IR sensors, ISR, electronic warfare, C4 systems, naval network computing |
| Integrated Mission Systems (IMS) — ~35% of DRS revenue | Mission Systems + Space Systems | Maritime electric power & propulsion, force protection, ship systems |

**C. Common Customers and Programs**
Both serve the U.S. Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force, and intelligence community. DRS is a Tier 1 supplier on programs where NOC is prime contractor. A key shared program is the **Columbia-class submarine**: DRS provides electric propulsion systems; NOC provides systems integration at the Mission Systems level.

**D. Same Macro Demand Drivers**
Rising global defense budgets, military modernization, demand for autonomous systems, advanced sensing, undersea warfare, and AI/ML-enabled platforms — both companies benefit identically.

**E. Revenue Mix**
- DRS: ~87% U.S. government revenue
- NOC: ~80%+ U.S. government revenue

**F. Industry Classification**
Both classified under GICS: **Industrials / Aerospace & Defense**. Regularly grouped together in sector peer analyses by investment banks.

**Key Framing:** NOC is a **prime contractor** (B-21, F-35 fuselage, Global Hawk, Sentinel ICBM); DRS is a **Tier 1 sub-system supplier** in defense electronics. DRS can be thought of as a pure-play proxy for NOC's Mission Systems segment — making it a tighter comparable than generalist defense primes.

### 4. DRS Beta — Notes for WACC / Comparables

**Observed Levered (Equity) Beta: ~0.16**
This is **not reliable** for two reasons:
1. Only ~2.5 years of trading history (since Nov 2022) — too short for a robust beta estimate
2. ~72% held by Leonardo S.p.A. (a single parent) limits the freely tradeable float, suppressing observed price volatility

**Capital Structure:**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Market Cap | ~$9.25–11.1B |
| Total Debt | ~$452M |
| Cash | ~$380M |
| Net Debt | ~$72M |
| Debt/Equity (market) | ~0.17–0.18 |
| Effective Tax Rate | ~21% |

**Unlevered Beta (Hamada equation):**
Unlevered Beta = 0.16 / [1 + (0.79 × 0.18)] = 0.16 / 1.14 ≈ **~0.14**
(This is almost certainly too low to be economically meaningful)

**Recommended Approach:**
Use **Damodaran's Aerospace & Defense sector unlevered beta (~0.70–0.85)** and re-lever using DRS's specific D/E ratio. This produces a far more defensible beta than the raw observed figure.

## PART 2 — NORTHROP GRUMMAN (NOC)

---

### 5. Why NOC Doesn't Appear to Report Interest Expense (and Where It Actually Is)

**Short answer:** NOC **does** report interest expense — it is a clearly labeled, standalone non-operating line item. The confusion arises from the **FAS/CAS pension accounting split**, which places a large offsetting item directly alongside interest expense, making the net "other income/expense" block appear near-zero.

**Income Statement Structure:**

```
Net Sales
− Operating Costs & Expenses (product, service, G&A, pension service cost, CAS pension)
= Operating Income (EBIT)
  [Below the line — non-operating:]
  − Interest Expense                  ~−$621M (FY2024)
  + Non-operating FAS Pension Benefit ~+$670M (FY2024)  ← offsets interest!
  +/− Other income/expense
= Earnings Before Income Taxes
− Income Tax Expense
= Net Earnings
```

**Recent Interest Expense Figures:**

| Period | Interest Expense |
|---|---|
| FY 2022 | ~$506M |
| FY 2023 | ~$545M |
| TTM June 2024 | ~$569M |
| FY 2024 | ~$621M |

**Why the confusion:** The **non-operating pension benefit (~+$670M in FY2024)** roughly cancels out the interest expense in the same section. This pension benefit fluctuates dramatically year to year due to mark-to-market (MTM) adjustments. The apparent **operating margin collapse in 2023 (~6.5%)** was primarily driven by a $1.17B B-21 charge and pension MTM losses — not a structural business deterioration.

**For WACC calculation:**
- Pre-tax cost of debt: ~$621M / ~$16,500M ≈ **3.8%**
- Effective tax rate: **~16.8%** (not 21%; NOC benefits from R&D credits and pension deductions)
- After-tax cost of debt: 3.8% × (1 – 0.168) ≈ **3.2%**

#### c. Terminal Growth Rate

For a mature, large-cap U.S. defense contractor, anchor the terminal growth rate to:
- Long-run U.S. nominal GDP growth: ~4–5% (real ~2% + inflation ~2%)
- Historical defense budget CAGR: ~3–5% nominal
- **Best practice:** terminal growth rate should not exceed long-run nominal GDP; set at or below real GDP growth + inflation target (~2.0–2.5%)

| Scenario | Terminal Growth Rate | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Bear | 1.5% | Fiscal austerity, sequestration, defense budget contraction |
| Base | 2.0–2.5% | In line with U.S. inflation target; appropriate for mature regulated defense contractor |
| Bull | 3.0% | Sustained elevated spending from geopolitical pressures — use with caution; at 3% with WACC ~8%, the spread narrows significantly, making valuation very sensitive |

> **Important:** Terminal value typically represents **60–80% of total DCF enterprise value**. A 0.5% change in terminal growth rate can move enterprise value by ~10–15%. Always present a two-way sensitivity table (WACC × g).
